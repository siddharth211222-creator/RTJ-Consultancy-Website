Mentorship built on lived experience, not a template.

Raushan mentors the way she wishes she'd been mentored — as someone who has sat on both sides of the admissions table, and who has personally gone through the process she now guides applicants through.

Raushan's mentoring philosophy is rooted in one conviction: applicants are most competitive when they stop trying to sound like an ideal candidate and start communicating who they actually are. Admissions committees read thousands of statements built from the same formulas. What they remember is a genuine story, clearly told.

Her role isn't to hand applicants a script. It's to help them find the story they already have — and give it the strategic shape a competitive application needs.

That belief is grounded in her own path. Raushan pursued her LL.M. at the University of Cambridge as a Commonwealth Shared Cambridge Trust Scholar, was admitted to Stanford Law School, and was selected as one of only two researchers worldwide for a highly competitive post-doctoral research position at Friedrich-Alexander-Universität Erlangen-Nürnberg. She has personally navigated the essays, the interviews, the funding applications, and the waiting — which is why her mentorship focuses as much on the applicant's nerves and self-doubt as it does on the paperwork.

01

Authenticity over formula

No templated personal statements. Every applicant's narrative is built from their own experience, not a generic mould of what committees are assumed to want.

02

Strategy alongside story

A compelling story still has to be positioned well — against the right programmes, the right scholarships, and the right version of the applicant's own trajectory.

03

Honesty about the odds

Direct, sometimes difficult, guidance about what's realistic — delivered early enough that it's still useful, not after an application has already been submitted.

How Mentorship Actually Works

An end-to-end process, not a single edit pass

Every applicant enters at a different stage, but the full mentorship covers each of these — in sequence, and at the pace the applicant needs.

1

Where you stand

An honest profile assessment — academic record, work experience, and goals — to understand what's realistic and where the gaps are.

2

Building the list

University and programme selection based on genuine fit, not prestige alone — balancing reach, match, and safety options across the right countries and programme types.

3

Profile building

Identifying what strengthens the application before it's even written — additional experience, positioning, or framing that makes the eventual narrative more credible.

4

Personal statements & SOPs

Drafting and rewriting the core narrative until it reads as genuinely the applicant's own — not a template, and not Raushan's voice standing in for theirs.

5

CV & résumé development

Reshaping academic and professional history into a document that reads clearly to an admissions committee, not just to the applicant themselves.

6

Recommendation letter strategy

Guidance on who to ask, what to brief them with, and how to make sure each letter adds something the rest of the application doesn't already say.

7

Scholarship & funding applications

Treating funding as part of the admissions strategy from the outset, not an afterthought — merit scholarships, tuition waivers, fellowships, and assistantships alike.

8

Interview preparation & career planning

Practising the interview itself, and thinking beyond admission to how the programme fits into the applicant's longer-term direction.

What Sets It Apart

A Cambridge LL.M. on a Commonwealth Shared Cambridge Trust Scholarship, a Stanford Law admit, and one of two researchers selected worldwide for her current post-doctoral position — she has recently gone through exactly the kind of competitive process her mentees are navigating.

Academically active

Cross-disciplinary

Direct, not distant
